## Introduction to Business Process Modelling

Business Process Modelling (BPMN) describes the flow of Work tasks, Decisions and Stakeholders (ABPMP International 2019). It can be used to describe processes and knowledge of process complexity can enhance organisation’s working processes. With BPMN, analysis and redesign (OMG 2025) can be done through processes and it is possible to interpret and evaluate the BPMN (OMG 2025). This e-portfolio explores how concepts, models and examples contribute to understanding the role of modelling in organisational improvement whilst BPMN provides an organisational level of clarity, efficiency and informed decisions.

## Core BPM Concepts Related to Business Process Modelling

In order to have access and control of components, seven concepts in business process modelling have been applied: end-to-end visibility, standardised notation, process transparency, AS IS and TO BE Modelling, Process Alignment with Organisational Strategy, Identification of Inefficiencies and Bottlenecks, Cross Functional Interaction and Collaboration (Galea 2026). Because the BPMN is an OMG standard it is a set of rules to ensure that it is possible for different analysts, managers and technical staff members to understand a process in the same way. For any modelling of a TO‑BE process, it is important that organisations have the basics of the AS‑IS process in their grasp, such as: events, activities, gateways, handoffs etc. Modelling has another strategic function: how the processes relate to the organisational goals (ABPMP International 2019). By collaborating with two other organisations - act of visualisation, reach of their decision points and the cross functional interaction, BPMN helps the companies to identify their efficiencies and shoreline improvements.

## BPMN Best Practices (Scholarly Article)

## Summary
In this article Recker & Mendling share their experiences and insights around the challenges and best practices for the current, BPMN modelling practice. The authors explain how BPMN has grown to deal with complex organisational-based processes, like those with multi‑actor workflows and event‑driven behaviour. They emphasize the need to be careful in building a model, and of what its consequences might be in terms of inefficiency and misuse if it is used again. Also, it outlines on a single example how to engage a process mining tool with BPMN and concludes by posing a question as to whether to consider BPMN as a true representation of how the system will behave or not. Increase the quality of modelling: Discuss common modelling problems and explain them: e.g., using a wrong gateway; modelling exception flows, etc.

## Example of Business Process Modelling

A representation of an implementation of the business processes can be a model of organisation's workflow to get an invoice approved. There were a significant number of manual checks, inconsistencies in decision making, and handoffs that were identified in the AS-Is model. This process could be followed through the BPMN and demonstrate the repetitive approval processes which analysts could observe. For the TO-BE process model: the condition of gateway were well specified and the transition movement were smooth, integrating the conditions for validating the process were automised (Bizagi 2025). This resulted in faster approvals, less mistakes and better visibility of employees. In this case, we see how we can apply BPMN to help discover inefficiencies and to make better processes for improved accuracy, speed and user satisfaction.

## BPMN Example (Bizagi Model)

## Summary
This artefact is a Bizagi Modeler (2025) BPMN of a Customer Refund process. Events, activities, gateway and message flows show how an "order refund request" finishes its journey through the various processes within the "Customer service", "Financial" and "Approval" workflows (Bizagi 2025). The following challenges have been emphasized in the model: unclear decision rules and manual checks steps. Also displays exception flows for rejected flows as well as escalation flows. When using Modelling, the design produced by the activity will be visible in the diagram created, which means for example, that if the design is inefficient, this can be identified from the design because the diagram can be easily analysed.
