# COIT20252-Business-Process-Management

## Introduction to Process Analysis

The one important phase in BPM Life Cycle is process analysis that ensures the currently running processes and their performance in achieving the organisation's goals. It comprises studying activities, handoffs, business rules and errors, risks and opportunities for improvement (ABPMP, 2019, p.130). This portfolio is a product of the usage of process analysis worked out structurally, analysis tools, real-life examples, proof of learning and reflection with authentic artefacts.

## Core BPM Concepts Related to Process Analysis

End-to-end process thinking, management based on performance and continuous improvement are the core principles of BPM that underlie process analysis. Business Process Management is a weekly basis process oriented programme, which aims to measure, monitor and optimize over time (ABPMP, 2019, p.40). Organisations should all understand the process of the AS‑IS, the boundary of the AS-IS, its initial inputs, its output, the constraints of the process, and the interactions that the AS-IS has with cross-functional processes prior to bringing in a redesign. Analysis also aids to strategic alignment, as processes need to align to the goals of the organisation. Organisations can then understand why things are inefficient and what value adding activities they are taking place by examining hand-over, capacity and variation. Structured and evidence based process analysis is based upon these concepts.

## Methods and Tools for Process Analysis

There are a variety of approaches that can be used for effective process analysis. Process mining tools like Celonis or UiPath interpret system event logs to show real processes, rework loops and bottlenecks (Gartner, 2026, p.7). Analysts use traditional approaches such as discussions, work camps and observation to help them learn about informal working arrangements, cultural influences, and human interactions (ABPMP, 2019, p.138). Value-Stream Mapping, 5 Whys, and Fish-bone diagrams are common Lean tools that help identify waste and uncover root causes (Lean Enterprise Institute 2025, p.33). Performance measurement techniques like cycle time analysis, cost analysis, and variation analysis help to gain quantitative information about efficiency and capacity. Business rules analysis helps to explain and understand decision logic and compliance requirements. All these tools allow for structural analysis, based on evidence, instead of assumptions or ad-hoc judgements.

## Example of Process Analysis

An actual example of process analysis is to look at an organisation's customers onboarding process. The results of the AS-IS showed long flow time, as the documents have to be manually checked, there is no guideline handoff between departments and inconsistent understanding of the Business Rules. Process mining identified situations, where the documents were missing from the process and needed to be returned to the customer. Lean practices helped in identifying the waste caused by producing inefficient approvals. The TO‑BE recommendations included automated document verification, rules that are more clearly defined and handoffs that streamline. This meant that onboarding time was reduced, Customer Satisfaction was improved and compliance errors were reduced.
