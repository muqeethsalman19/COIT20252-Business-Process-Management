# COIT20252-Business-Process-Management

## Introduction to Process Analysis

Process Analysis is an important step in BPM Lifecycle where one learns the operation and effectiveness of a process in meeting the organisation's goals. It requires an analysis of activities, handoffs, business rules and performance measures to look for inefficiencies, risks and improvement opportunities (ABPMP, 2019, p.130). The application of process analysis, its structured approaches, its analytical tools and the real-life examples of this applied analysis are explored through this portfolio, with artefacts of authenticity and reflections about my learning.

## Core BPM Concepts Related to Process Analysis

End-to-end process thinking, management based on performance and continuous improvement are the core principles of BPM that underlie process analysis. BPM operates on a weekly basis with a focus on processes as a resource that has to be measured, monitored and optimized over time (ABPMP, 2019, p.40). Organisations need to have grasp of the AS‑IS process, its inputs, outputs, constraints and interactions with cross-functional processes before bringing in a redesign. Analysis also aids to strategic alignment, as processes need to align to the goals of the organisation. Organisations can then better understand inefficiencies and value-adding activities by studying variation, capacity and hand‑overs. These concepts are the basis of structured and evidence based process analysis.

## Methods and Tools for Process Analysis

There are several ways of achieving an effective process analysis. Process mining tools like Celonis and UiPath can be used to help identify in-the-real world process flows, bottlenecks, and rework loops (Gartner, 2026, p.7). Analysts are able to learn about human interaction, culture, and informal work practices through traditional methods, such as interviews, workshops, and direct observation (ABPMP, 2019, p.138). Lean tools, such as value stream mapping, 5 Whys, and fishbone diagrams, help you pinpoint waste and understand the root cause (Lean Enterprise Institute, 2025, p.33). A variety of tools are available to measure the performance including cycle time analysis, cost analysis and variation analysis which give quantitative information concerning efficiency and capacity. Business rules analysis, which utilizes rules to define business logic and compliance requirements, is the focus of the situation. Combined, they provide for structured analysis and not mere guesses or untargeted judgment.

## Example of Process Analysis

An actual example of process analysis is to look at an organisation's customers' onboarding process. The AS-Is revealed quite long flow time due to manual checking of documents, absence of guideline handoff between departments, and inconsistent understanding of business rules. Process mining revealed that there were situations, in which customers had to return missing documents. Lean practices enabled waste to be identified in inefficient approval processes. The TO‑BE recommendations included automated document verification, rules that are more clearly defined and handoffs that streamline. This meant that onboarding time was reduced, Customer Satisfaction was improved and compliance errors were reduced. Further discussion of how structured analysis can help you identify root causes and provide guidance for customer experience-driven redesign to create a better, more efficient experience for your customers.
